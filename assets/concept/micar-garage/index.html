the card

Your car, slabbed

  • A

    The label

    Grade, tier, the car, the serial, and a QR that opens the car's page. Printed on both sides, like a real slab.

  • B

    The shine is earned

    Foil intensity follows the grade. A 9.8 Showroom Miata throws rainbows; a 5.4 Daily is matte chrome. You can tell the grade from across the room.

  • C

    The case tells the story

    The card's back is the garage door, the same on every card. The case's rear label carries the chart: your car against the model's curve and its average sold price, with every service, mod and mileage update on the line.

  • D

    Raw until slabbed, then minted once

    A parked car is a raw card. Three entries later it goes in the case, gets its first grade, and is minted with a set, a number and its stamps. The grade can move. The minting never does.

engine note

Every entry stores two things: the value the engine computed that day, and the inputs it used (miles, condition, market index, trim price). When the engine improves, history is never rewritten; the car just gets a new entry. That is what makes the trail worth something to the next owner. Sample garage; figures are illustrative.

it rides with the card

Half of a car's Octane transfers with the card when it trades. A documented car is worth more as a card too, which is the whole point: the trail is the thing of value, and it stays with the car.

Rung 1 · now

Points

Octane lives in our database. No cash value, not transferable outside the lot. Zero regulatory weight, full incentive value.

Rung 2 · next

Anchored provenance

Each ledger entry's fingerprint is anchored to a public chain, so a buyer can verify a car's trail without trusting us. Still no token.

Rung 3 · maybe

A coin

A transferable OCT token tied to garage activity. The moment it can be bought and sold it may be a security, so this rung needs counsel first and a product people already love.
